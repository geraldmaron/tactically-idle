# Psychology

How people on a call think, how a negotiation moves them, and how the call can work on the
player. Use it while you fill the person cards (SKILL.md section 3), shape the stages (sections 5
and 6) and plan the escalation palette and temptation ([dilemma-patterns.md](dilemma-patterns.md)).
Sources are tagged from [sources.md](sources.md). All of it serves a call the player meets in
about ninety seconds, so each idea ends in a field it lands in.

## 1. The person, from the inside

Fill these for every key person, not only the subject. A courier, an owner or a child who is
only a stake leaves the call one voice short.

| Line | What it holds | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Want | What they say they want, in their words | It drives the first exchange |
| Wound | The thing that happened to them that the want answers | It gives the want its heat, and the player can find it |
| Self-lie | The story that keeps them right in their own eyes | Everyone believes they are justified [TV3]. The self-lie is what a good negotiator never attacks head on |
| Need, or hook | What would actually move them: to be believed, witnessed, in control, to keep face, to protect someone | Negotiators look for what the person values and build on it [AU16] |
| Breaking point | What turns them, as behavior | Humiliation, a loss of control, a deadline passing, the crowd, someone they love on the line |
| Voice | Two or three diction markers and one line only they would say | The blind voice test (D4) reads it |

The self-lie and the need are almost never the same. The taker says he wants a signed page (want)
because the owner called him a thief in front of the regulars (wound). He tells himself he only
wants the paper (self-lie). What would move him is the owner looking at him and saying she was
wrong (need). A call that gives the player a path to the need, at a cost, has depth. A call that
only trades on the want is a vending machine.

A person's distorted thinking is a lever, never a diagnosis. All-or-nothing beliefs ("without
this job nobody will care about me") drive extreme reactions, and a negotiator can reframe a
liability as an asset: "Six years at that counter. You're someone who shows up." [AU16] The text
shows the belief through what the person says and does. It never names a disorder (E4.8).

## 2. The negotiation as drama

The Behavioral Change Stairway Model climbs from active listening to empathy, rapport, influence
and then behavioral change [AU3]. A call that lets the player climb it, and fall off it, has a
spine. Stage 1 listens, stage 2 earns rapport or loses it, and stage 3 asks for the change.

The eight active listening skills make strong choice verbs, because each one has a risk [AU16]:

| Skill | Choice it makes | Its risk |
|---|---|---|
| Emotion label ("You sound like nobody believed you.") | Name what they feel | A wrong label lands as pity or as a judgment, and empathy is not pity [AU16] |
| Paraphrase | Say back what happened | It can restate the accusation |
| Mirror ("Pushed around?") | Repeat their last words | Overused, it sounds like a script |
| Effective pause | Say nothing and let the silence work | The silence belongs to the subject. It costs minutes, and the people held pay them |
| Minimal encouragers | Keep them talking | It buys time and no movement |
| "I" message | Say how their words land on you | It makes the negotiator a person, and a target |
| Summary | Read the whole account back | One wrong word starts the anger again |
| Open question | Ask about the wound | It reopens the wound |

Boundaries are drama too. A subject who says "If she gets hurt, it's on you" is testing who
controls the line. Taking the blame hands them the call. Refusing it plainly can cost rapport
now and buy respect later [AU16].

A face-saving exit is often the whole third stage. People come out when coming out lets them
stay who they think they are. The out-the-back, the walk past the tape, the page with their side
on it: each is a way out that keeps face, and each should cost someone else something.

## 3. What escalation is made of

One threat beat repeated is a call with one psychology. Draw the call's escalation from the
palette in [dilemma-patterns.md](dilemma-patterns.md). Each entry is a different reason a person
gets worse, so each one needs a different response:

- Shame spiral: a question touches the wound in front of others.
- The crowd: the street, a phone held up, a chant.
- The partner: someone beside the subject who pushes.
- Lost control: a door the subject didn't open, an officer they didn't see.
- A deadline passing: one they set themselves.
- The call from home: family on the line, for better or worse.
- Bargaining collapse: the deal they built falls apart.
- Withdrawal: the subject goes quiet and stops caring what happens.

## 4. The player's own psychology

The call can work on the player as well as on the people in it. Each lever below is a design
tool with an honest cost, never a trick that hides information.

- **Sunk cost.** People keep going once they have invested time, money or effort, and they rate
  their chances higher because of it [PS11]. Two hours into a hold, the player will want to keep
  holding. Make that a real choice: the hold that has cost hours now costs the owner's heart.
- **Commitment.** A promise the player made pulls on every later choice. Deception choices
  (pattern 15) live on it.
- **Anchoring a false read.** The first account frames everything after it. A briefing that is
  true but partial ("Patrol says he's armed and angry") anchors one reading of the subject that a
  stage 2 reveal can overturn (L7). The anchor must be fair: a reasonable read of what was said,
  never a lie the call told.
- **Loss framing.** A choice framed as what it could lose weighs more than the same choice framed
  as a gain [PS3]. Use it in previews to make the safe option feel costly.
- **The cost of being right.** A player who guessed the truth and acted on it should sometimes
  find that being right still hurt someone. That is the line they remember.
- **Peak and end.** People judge an experience by its sharpest moment and its end [PS4]. Each
  call needs one peak per path, and the epilogue is the end.
- **The curiosity gap.** One open question pulls the player through [PS1]. The reframe should
  answer it in a way that opens a second, smaller one, left for the epilogue.

## 5. Lies, and why the job avoids them

Negotiators are trained to make no promise command hasn't approved [AU6], and trust is what the
negotiation runs on [AU16]. That is exactly why a lie is a strong choice in a game. It is
tempting because it often works now. The subject relaxes, a hostage walks and a door opens. Its
cost is real because a discovered lie ends the trust every later step needs.

Write deception as the temptation pattern (dilemma pattern 15). The lie works in the moment, its
break is cued before it lands, and the break changes what the player can do next. The engine's
commitments carry it (`deception-commitments` in the backlog).

## 6. Limits

- No diagnosis, and no mental illness as the explanation for violence (E4.8).
- A crisis subject's self-harm risk follows E4 in full, whatever the tone tier.
- Psychology explains behavior to the writer. The player sees behavior, never the explanation
  (T11).
