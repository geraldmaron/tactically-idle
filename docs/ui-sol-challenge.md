## 1. Verdict in five sentences

After 60 seconds, I think I have opened a police administration app with a game buried inside it. I see currencies, rings, timers, portraits, badges, and warnings before I understand what my next decision changes. Marvel Snap establishes its central interaction immediately; this makes me complete intake, inspect a dashboard, read a briefing, and configure a deployment before reaching its central interaction. ## 2. Cross-cutting failures, ranked

### 1. The decision is below the paperwork

The live call’s choices sit after the header, stream information, team, civilian statuses, narrative, dialogue, and an empty radio panel. That makes the player scroll to discover what they can do while the interface claims the situation is urgent.

**Reference pattern:** Reigns keeps the dilemma and the gesture that resolves it together. Into the Breach keeps the threatened outcome and the available intervention on the same board.

**Replacement:** Build the live screen around one visible situation and its available actions. Put the decision above the fold. Make background information expandable from the people, room, or evidence it concerns.

### 2. Every component has the same visual authority

Blue rounded containers, borders, gradients, pills, icons, and section headings appear everywhere. The player must read to distinguish an emergency, a recommendation, a transaction, an explanation, and a historical record.

**Reference pattern:** Apple Wallet gives the active pass a dominant silhouette. Brawl Stars separates the primary play action from surrounding management. Balatro distinguishes cards, modifiers, and scoring through position and behavior.

**Replacement:** Establish distinct treatments for:

- **Situation:** people and place.
- **Decision:** interactive options with consequences.
- **Management:** compact operational rows.
- **Receipt:** changes that just occurred.

Stop enclosing every paragraph in a card.

### 3. The first session teaches administration before agency

The first-use sequence presents content preferences, headquarters systems, a call catalogue, objectives, background, biographies, equipment, starting position, and optional vehicles. The player has no experience against which to value most of that information.

**Reference pattern:** Clash Royale’s opening battles teach through constrained action. Duolingo introduces the lesson interaction before exposing the full progression system.

**Replacement:** After a short content notice, take the player into one authored call with an assigned team and prepared kit. Introduce hiring, recovery, construction, and equipment when the call creates a reason to use them.

### 4. The blueprint occupies space without carrying the decision

The map is repeatedly displayed during briefing and deployment, then shrinks into an overview during the result. Meanwhile, the consequential facts remain in prose: who is threatened, where Rodrigo stands, what the stream can see, and what an extraction would expose.

**Reference pattern:** Into the Breach makes location explain consequences. XCOM’s movement and targeting previews connect the selected action to the terrain.

**Replacement:** Use the map to show actionable spatial relationships: reported positions, confirmed positions, selected access point, extraction route, and exposure. Remove decorative architectural information from the default operational view.

### 5. The choice presentation encourages percentage shopping

The visible opening offers 90% success with low harm against 66% and 69% with moderate harm. The narrative supplies atmosphere, but the displayed numbers supply an obvious answer.

**Reference pattern:** Balatro makes choices depend on the player’s current configuration. Into the Breach makes protecting one objective compete with protecting another. Reigns makes decisions affect competing interests.

**Replacement:** Give each action a concrete advantage and a concrete cost. Public contact gains cooperation while increasing audience pressure. Extraction protects a civilian while risking detection. Observation gains evidence while consuming time. Show those exchanges before the player commits.

### 6. People are described more often than they are made relevant

Officers receive hobby sentences. Civilians receive long biographies. The interface rarely connects those details to an immediate relationship, preference, capability, or consequence.

**Reference pattern:** Hades makes character identity accumulate through encounters and responses. Football Manager Mobile connects a person’s condition and attributes to selection decisions.

**Replacement:** Surface personal information when it changes interpretation or action. Show who needs rest, who handled the previous negotiation, who knows this witness, and who is asking for support. Move unrelated biography into optional profiles.

### 7. Progress is expressed in too many unexplained visual languages

Portrait arcs, rank arcs, stress bars, department XP, officer XP, trust, stars, room pips, ownership counts, situation counts, and hourly funding compete for attention.

**Reference pattern:** Apple Fitness gives each ring a stable meaning. Clash Royale distinguishes account progression from a card’s progression through consistent placement and presentation.

**Replacement:** Give every recurring shape one meaning. Label department level, trust, stress, equipment condition, and availability explicitly. Remove progress graphics that do not answer a decision on that screen.

### 8. The idle loop lacks a visible return moment

HQ foregrounds an available call and administrative sections. It does not establish the emotional rhythm of returning to a department that has changed while the player was away.

**Reference pattern:** Idle Miner and AdVenture Capitalist make accumulated production and the next investment legible. Apple Fitness shows what changed and what remains.

**Replacement:** On return, summarize earnings, recovery, completed training, equipment service, and new calls. Offer one recommended next action based on those changes.

### 9. The reward language fights the stated value

The debrief foregrounds stars, funding, XP, and a camera multiplier. The line saying cameras made the result “count for 19% more” makes the interface sound as though a safe resolution has greater value when recorded.

**Reference pattern:** XCOM’s after-action presentation distinguishes personnel outcomes from strategic rewards. Hades separates the narrative consequence of an encounter from resource collection.

**Replacement:** Lead with people’s outcomes and the team’s condition. Present funding and progression afterward. Explain public accountability as a separate effect with its own cause.

## 4. The ten changes that would most change how the game feels

1. **Rebuild the live call around agency.** Following Reigns’ decision proximity, fit the immediate situation, approaching consequence, and available actions into the initial viewport.

2. **Turn the blueprint into an operational surface.** Following Into the Breach’s threat legibility, connect people, uncertainty, access points, and action previews directly to the plan.

3. **Replace the opening administration sequence with one authored call.** Following Clash Royale’s constrained introduction, assign the initial team and kit, then unlock management through consequences.

4. **Make choices exchange concrete advantages.** Following Balatro’s contextual decisions, give every action a distinct gain and sacrifice instead of one visibly dominant percentage.

5. **Create a return-to-department moment.** Following idle-game collection patterns, summarize earnings, recovery, completed work, and new incidents with one recommended next action.

6. **Reduce HQ to decisions and exceptions.** Following Brawl Stars’ primary-action hierarchy, show one recommended call and only the management issues requiring attention.

7. **Give the visual system four distinct jobs.** Following Wallet’s object hierarchy, differentiate situations, decisions, operational lists, and receipts through structure before adding decoration.

8. **Make personnel management answer readiness questions.** Following Football Manager Mobile, replace default portrait galleries with comparable rows showing availability, condition, role coverage, and recovery.

9. **Rebuild debrief around people, then consequences, then resources.** Following XCOM’s after-action separation, resolve every person’s state before presenting progression and funding.

10. **Unify construction, staffing, training, and equipment dependencies.** Following Two Point Hospital’s management relationships, show exactly what a purchase enables, what remains required, and who benefits.

## 5. What to delete outright

- **“We don’t check your age.”** Wallet’s contextual explanations provide the model: explain the current decision.
- **“Open in lab” from player-facing screens.** Linear keeps internal machinery outside the user’s task.
- **Large empty radio cards.** Linear’s compact empty activity states avoid reserving a panel for nothing.
- **The permanent standard-kit gallery during preparation.** XCOM’s loadout emphasis belongs on changeable, consequential equipment.
- **Simultaneous near-identical call cards.** Hades’ encounter variation is the reference for making another encounter worth inspecting.
- **Hobby paragraphs in operational squad and patrol lists.** Football Manager Mobile’s comparison views prioritize selection information.
- **Unlabeled progress bars, portrait arcs, and room pips.** Fitness’ stable measurement language is the standard.
- **Generic “Useful” labels.** Into the Breach’s explicit effect previews replace unexplained recommendations.
- **“The cost” and “The pull” as repeated choice-review headings.** 80 Days’ concrete circumstances provide the reference.
- **The casebook embedded beneath the dispatch queue.** Hades’ Codex shows how accumulated knowledge can have its own destination.
- **Repeated full-size locked catalogues inside active tasks.** Clash Royale separates preparation from future collection possibilities.

- **The unprofitable grant as the sole product on a dedicated tab.** Idle Miner’s investment loop requires an actionable economic proposition.
