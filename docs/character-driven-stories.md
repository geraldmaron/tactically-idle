# Character-driven incident stories

This branch replaces generic process narration with finite, authored stories. New stories use a new content version. Previously issued calls and their saved outcomes stay readable and unchanged.

## First playable specimen: One Last Signature

A courier, Ben Flores, and print-shop owner Mara Holt are being held by her former employee Lewis. Lewis wants a statement clearing him of alleged theft. The allegation is not an established fact. Ben's unsigned delivery slip connects the opening to the ending.

The central early choice concerns Ben's phone: release him immediately with it, spend time asking to leave it with Mara, or use the squad's actual qualified communication equipment. Releasing him remains real progress. Contact with Mara determines when the player learns why the original demand is still unresolved. A later agreement must concern something the team can actually offer; a misunderstanding creates a specific clarification route. Any urgent protection option needs a current danger cue and the actual assigned equipment and qualifications.

The story is scoped to a business location. A patrol report cannot invent a direct quote. Later knowledge stays hidden until the relevant conversation or observation occurs. Promises and care are reflected in endings only when their effects were committed.

## Content and pacing requirements

- Name people, their immediate wants, and a concrete unanswered question in the opening.
- Make every required committed choice change evidence, a relationship or commitment, a person's situation, a real tradeoff, or the available ending.
- Do not roll or charge operation time for changing a menu or entering the next stage.
- Introduce a later discovery that changes what the player should consider.
- Make complications and setbacks name the obstacle. A revised choice must respond to that obstacle.
- Keep equipment causal and contextual. Owning a tool does not settle a person's intentions.
- Author coherent whole-scene variants rather than independently recombining contradictory motives and facts.
- Keep outcome leads concise. Show meaningful changes by default and preserve the complete saved record in details.
- Resolve each person's actual safety and care state. Do not invent arrests, confessions, injuries, calls, returned property, or fulfilled promises.

## Six distinct patterns

Report checks should change the player's understanding; medical assistance should hinge on a concrete obstacle to accepted care; protective incidents should turn on a specific agreement. Armed incidents separate ending the danger from helping a person still affected by it. Hostage incidents change after one person leaves. Rescues change when the team learns what assistance the person needs.

The first specimen receives a transcript and effect review before these standards are applied to the remaining patterns. This is an implementation checkpoint, not a published story release.

## Compatibility and validation

Before edits, the published v4 baseline was captured across 141 complete generated definitions, all six current practice IDs and four seeded board-selection streams. The new content must preserve those fingerprints, active and pending runs, immutable outcome text, rewards, inventory reservations and all ten campaign slots.

Validation must include coherent full, partial and setback transcripts; public-information-only previews; actual equipment and qualification alternatives; injury and care interruptions; no repeated reward farming; and phone-sized interaction checks. Numeric test counts alone do not establish story quality.
