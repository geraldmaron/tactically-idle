# Game UX direction: visual-first surfaces and Command Staff

**Status:** visual surfaces and Command Staff v1 are implemented. The three managers are on HQ. Work still to do lives only in [docs/backlog.md](backlog.md).

## The problem

Most surfaces explained themselves in sentences. Develop was a 4,450px column of 20 near-identical text cards ("Unlocks purchase: X · Unlocks purchase: Y"). Gear put a five-column number table and a full-width buy button on every item. Debriefs were a title, a chip and a run-on line ("Objective Follow-Up Agreed · Civilians Everyone Safe"). The game already owns painted portraits and gear art, but most screens didn't use them.

## Patterns we follow

These are the conventions of current mobile management and idle games (collection grids, tech trees, manager rosters, result cards):

1. **Art first, words second.** Each thing has a face: a portrait, gear art, or an emblem. The name is the caption, not the content.
2. **Numbers become shapes.** Use a count badge (×6), a condition strip, a score ring, or tier pips. Exact values live in the detail sheet and in accessible labels.
3. **One tile, one tap.** A tile opens its sheet. Secondary actions (restock, service, scrap) live in that sheet, not on every tile.
4. **Attention is a pip.** An alert dot, a glow on whatever you can buy now, and a dimmed lock on what you can't.
5. **Progress per collection.** Each branch or set shows owned / total as a bar.
6. **Caps and no bleed.** At most five operations on the board. Nothing scrolls sideways or gets cut off at a screen edge.

### Build-tab patterns, challenged

Each familiar pattern was argued against before it was built. The verdicts:

- **Welcome-back sheet.** Strongest failure: a modal on every return nags, and one that opens mid-call buries the scene the player came back to. Alternative: keep the report as an HQ card only, which a player landing on Ops never sees. Verdict: accepted with controls. It opens only on load or after the page was hidden for at least the settlement's own 15-minute report threshold, never when a course finishing mid-play adds to the report; one Continue clears it; closing it keeps the HQ card. No Collect button, because the money was applied when the time settled and a button that pretends otherwise is a lie.
- **Level badge on officer cards.** Strongest failure: officers have no level, so a level number would invent a stat and push the roster toward loot. Alternative: no badge, growth only in the profile. Verdict: accepted with controls. The badge shows the role's real main rating and its ring the real XP toward the next point (`officerGrowth`, the same numbers as the profile), and the tile says both aloud. No rarity frames (E3).
- **Upgrade buttons in Develop.** Strongest failure: a one-tap buy on a scrolling list spends permanent funding on a mis-tap, and nothing undoes it, when a milestone might have granted it free. Alternative: the idle-game buy button that buys on tap. Verdict: accepted with controls. The button looks and reads like the research-lab Upgrade it is, but the tap opens the sheet that confirms the spend, the pattern strategy games use for permanent research.
- **Armory shop grid.** Strongest failure: a gold price on every tile turns the Armory into a store front and hides what the department already holds. Alternative: price only in the sheet. Verdict: accepted. Owned count, condition and attention stay on the same tile, and the tag's colour says whether a buy is possible now, which is the one decision the grid serves.
- **Three-mark grade.** Found in the sweep: the marks came from the safety score, which does not count the team's own force, so a call closed by taking a man by hand with shots fired earned three marks. Verdict: rejected as built and fixed. Force, a partial close or an agreed follow-up caps the grade at two, and an unresolved or incomplete call at one (found by the Codex adversarial review); talking everyone out to a resolved close is the only way to three.

### Progression pass, challenged

- **Milestone grants.** Strongest failure: a free grant on the same tier the player is saving for makes buying feel like a waste, so players stop buying and wait. Alternative: grants as discounts. Verdict: accepted with controls. Price and milestone sit side by side so the player sees the trade (cash now, or wait for Level 5), milestones sit well past the start (nothing is granted on a new campaign) and later tiers ask for more. No fake Claim button: a met milestone has already applied, and the toast says so.
- **Rank on the card.** Strongest failure: a rank badge becomes a level badge and turns officers into loot. Alternative: rank only in the profile. Verdict: accepted with controls. Ranks are real US police ranks, earned from the work, capped, and they read as a title ("Sgt. Brooks"), not a number. No rarity frames (E3). The ring is real XP toward the next rank.
- **Two ranks above Corporal only.** Captain was considered and rejected: the player commands the unit, and a Captain under the player would outrank the Command Staff. Senior Officer was rejected as a sixth step that adds a threshold and no new decision.
- **Rank traits.** Strongest failure: every sergeant becomes a mentor and traits stop telling officers apart. Verdict: accepted with controls. The trait is earned on promotion with its cause recorded and shown ("Earned on promotion to Sergeant, after ..."), an officer holds at most three, starting officers keep the traits they were built with, and the next rank's trait shows on the ladder before it lands (the swat-officer-stories telegraph).

### Depth register surfaces, challenged

The depth fields from the scenario-depth work are optional, and a call without them renders as before.

- **Cold open** (`DepthBrief.tsx`). Set as unclamped prose on Prepare above the briefing, and at the top of the live briefing the first time it opens. After that it folds to "Before the call". It never sits on the live scene's first screen, which belongs to the moment and the choices.
- **Who they are.** The first section of the full briefing, above why the team was sent, on Prepare and live.
- **Weighed choices.** Edited 2026-10-21: the owner found the expand toggle plus a separate Review button clunky, so the card is one target again. Strongest failure: the pull drops off the card, so a player who never opens a choice never reads it. Alternative: keep expand-in-place. Verdict: accepted with controls. The card shows the title, the cost, who leads, any command line, the odds, the time and possible harm (harm and lead are never folded). One tap opens the review sheet, which carries the pull, the cost and the full summary. Locked cards still open it. The card's name is "Review <title>" and it is described by its odds and harm line; focus moves to the sheet's title and returns to the card on close, Escape closes it, and the page keeps its scroll.
- **What matters now** (2026-10-21, removed the same day). One block above the cards held the stage's threat and the source pulling hardest on the odds. The owner chose to have nothing said twice: the threat is in the strip, and each source's ticker now says in words which checks it makes harder. See [ops-experience.md](ops-experience.md), "The decide beat".
- **A still page while a decision is open** (2026-10-21). Live elements never move the rest of the page: the comms log holds its box before the first line and scrolls inside it, the threat pill keeps its shape when the threat lands ("Now") and what happened joins the comms log, where new lines join at the bottom and scroll it so no shown line moves, live numbers use tabular digits padded to a fixed width, the card's odds bar stacks its bands from the left edge, and the stream's chat holds a two-line box. Boxes are sized in the text's own units, so larger text grows them instead of clipping. Below 520px of height (a phone at 200% text) the strip scrolls with the page instead of pinning. Checked with a layout-shift observer at 390x844 and at 195x422 with reduced motion and long names: no shift through count changes, the first comms lines and the threat landing.
- **Results and endings** run long now. Neither the decision card nor the debrief hero clamps them.

## How the game reveals more

One rule per kind of hidden content (disclosure-taxonomy, 2026-10-19):

- **Always visible:** harm, losses and civilian outcomes. Never behind a control.
- **Disclosure** (`src/ui/components/Disclosure.tsx`): evidence and logs inline, such as why an outcome happened, the decision record, odds factors, the full briefing and extra scene conditions. One summary row with a count and a chevron.
- **Sheet:** reference that is not about this moment (the map key) and anything with its own actions (an item, an officer, a hire, finances).
- **Chip rail:** filters (the Armory, the casebook). Always visible, never folded.
- **Show all:** long lists keep a plain button (HQ results, decision choices).

## Command Staff v1

Command Staff is a visible roster on HQ. Hiring is a Develop purchase. Each manager has a card and detail sheet with policy controls and an activity log. Managers never make tactical choices, and automation has an off switch.

The strongest failure mode is automation removing the decisions that make the game interesting. The current scope is accepted with controls: managers automate department routines with visible policies and logs. Peer Support Lead, Recruiter and the scope of routine patrol work belong to the [backlog owner decisions](backlog.md#owner-decisions).

The built roster has three managers. The Watch Commander runs duty rotation.

| Manager | Hire | Salary | Policy | Automation (each clock hour, inside the 24h accrual window) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Watch Commander (`watch_commander`) | Develop `personnel_watch_commander`, $1,600 or free at 5 calls resolved | $45/h while on | Rest limit (30-80, default 60), per-squad manual opt-out | Rests an idle squad when any member reaches the limit; returns it to its earlier duty (Patrol if unknown) once everyone is below 30. Uses the setSquadDuty rules, so deployed squads are never touched. |
| Training Sergeant (`training_sergeant`) | Develop `personnel_training_sergeant` (needs Training academy), $1,600 or free at level 4 | $45/h while on | Course (unlocked courses only), funding reserve (default $2,500) | Fills free training places with the lowest-stress eligible officer below 30 stress, using the startCourse rules, only if funding after the fee stays at or above the reserve. |
| Quartermaster (`quartermaster`) | The existing equipment manager node | None | Hourly service ceiling | Unchanged equipment-manager servicing; on/off is the service budget. Its log records servicing and restock spend. |

Code: `src/sim/command-staff.ts` (state, commands `setManagerEnabled` / `setManagerPolicy`, settlement hook, save validation), `src/ui/components/CommandStaff.tsx` (HQ roster and sheet), `src/ui/art/ManagerArt.tsx` (portrait with silhouette fallback). Only current-version saves load; old saves are rejected. Balance values are tunable.
