# Scenario depth

The calls are the game's main draw. This document says why the first four calls fall short of
that, what the owner decided on 2026-10-19, and how calls are held to it from now on. The work
itself is tracked in `docs/backlog.md` (`depth-bible`, `call-surfaces`, `deception-commitments`,
`hostage-depth-pilot`, then the three model-first calls).

## The finding

One Last Signature, Behind the Bathroom Door, After the Noise and My Chair Comes Too are correct.
They are honest, safe and inside budget, and the separate-reader review of the hostage pilot
closed 44 defects. They are also forgettable. Every gate measured correctness, and no gate could
fail a call for being dull. The text showed six causes:

1. **One threat beat.** Nearly every adverse result became "the handgun comes up at the owner".
   It was generated by one helper and reworded about fifteen times. Escalation had one psychology.
2. **Truths that switch behaviour and never reframe.** `lets_go`, `raised` and `knows_son` decide
   whether someone acts. None of them changes what an earlier scene meant.
3. **People with one want and no voice.** The taker had three direct lines in ten thousand
   words. The owner and the courier were only reported. The negotiator, who is the player's own
   voice, never spoke.
4. **Tactics, not values.** Most choices traded minutes against odds. Few made two reasonable
   players argue about what was right.
5. **House style rules that suppressed suspense.** These were the caps on speech tags and
   fragments, and the accusation word list applied to a character's own words.
6. **Endings that stopped at status.** There was no after.

## Decisions (owner, 2026-10-19)

- **New surfaces, not bigger budgets.** Each screen keeps its tight budget. A radio transcript
  carries the voices, an epilogue carries the after, and the board hook gets three lines.
- **The player can lie.** A lie or a promise command never approved is a real choice. It
  usually works in the moment and costs later, and the cost is cued before it lands.
- **Darker.** Harsher, specific consequences are shown: a subject can die on the card, injuries
  last, families carry it, and officers are changed by it. Three rules stay, because they exist
  for reasons other than taste:
  - The self-harm safe-messaging rules (E4) reduce real-world harm.
  - E2 (force is never the reward) is the game's identity.
  - E3 (identity never signals danger) prevents real-world prejudice.
  Wound anatomy stays off the card, because it adds no psychology.
- **Bible first, then one pilot, then an owner read.** The rules go into the four `swat-*`
  skills. One Last Signature is redesigned under them. The owner reads three paths aloud. No
  other call is rewritten before that read.

## How depth is held

The depth gates (D1 to D9) live in `swat-writing-review`. A call can fail them with zero defects.
The lenses, the psychology reference and the temptation and escalation patterns live in
`swat-call-design`. The budgets for the new surfaces live in `swat-call-prose`. Makers and works
are named only as the source of a technique, never as a voice to imitate.

A human taste anchor sits above every gate. The owner reads a pilot aloud and picks the lines
worth quoting, and those picks calibrate the reviewer. Without that read, a model writing and a
model reviewing will agree on competent prose and every gate will pass.
