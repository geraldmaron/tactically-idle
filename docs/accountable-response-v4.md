# Accountable response and incident variety

This release gives newly generated calls a specific reason for the tactical team's involvement, named support responsibilities, and outcomes that distinguish completed work from unresolved tasks. It is a fictional management model. The sources below support the roles and mission categories; they do not constitute practitioner validation of the game or its balance.

## Player contract

- The deployed team keeps responsibility when it requests help. A request is not a completed call.
- Emergency medical care belongs to a named medical service. Relevant clinical consultation can help with a care plan; it does not replace the team's communication skills or automatically take command.
- Support moves through requested, available and accepted states. Arrival uses visible operation time, not a real-time purchase timer. Other decisions can be made while waiting.
- Completed care accepted by the appropriate service can be a successful outcome. Merely forwarding an unresolved concern cannot earn full completion credit.
- A setback can lead to a revised plan. It does not automatically summon an unnamed superior team.
- Refuted reports can be closed. A person's communication needs or identity never establish danger.

## Bounded scope

The three existing decision families receive several seeded premises. Changes must affect information, responsibilities, preparation, service availability, timing or outcomes, rather than only the name or scenery. Ordinary-looking calls explain an independently justified high-risk dispatch, a reassessment of that report, or remaining work at an already deployed scene. Premises requiring unsupported large-event or multi-person simulation are deferred.

The player sees a small set of context-relevant choices. Preparation and consultations are bounded, and another attempt requires a changed plan or new information. Necessary care is not a paid equipment unlock. No new real-world assault, weapon or explosive instructions are part of this system.

## Compatibility

New behavior is keyed by incident content version 4. Issued version 1–3 incidents, authored Maple Street scenarios and existing practice IDs remain readable and retain their original actions, effects, random samples and settlement rules. New practice IDs demonstrate the revised response model. Campaign slots and ongoing operations are not reset.

Version 4 endings record an explicit disposition and whether the team's responsibility was completed. Full completion development-point and career credit require that completion evidence. Partial service, safety and information still receive honest credit. Old settlement formulas remain unchanged.

## Verification requirements

1. Requesting support never ends the incident or awards completion by itself.
2. Arrival and acceptance are deterministic, saved, validated and duplicate-safe. The wrong or unavailable service cannot accept responsibility.
3. Appropriate accepted care and non-force resolutions can earn full credit; unresolved high-safety exits cannot earn full completion development points.
4. Every bounded low-resource route reaches an honest ending, without repeated-roll farming or hidden-fact leaks.
5. Old scenario fingerprints, pending rewards, saves, random state and ten-slot isolation remain unchanged.
6. Hosted 320- and 390-pixel journeys verify request, waiting, continued decisions, acceptance, setback recovery and clear final outcomes.

- [Seattle Health One](https://www.seattle.gov/fire/safety-and-community/mobile-integrated-health/health-one): non-emergency care, referrals and follow-up.
- [NYC mobile crisis teams](https://www.nyc.gov/site/doh/health/health-topics/crisis-emergency-services-mobile-crisis-teams.page): assessment and care support, distinct from immediate hospitalization.
- [DOJ effective communication guidance](https://www.ada.gov/resources/law-enforcement-guide/): communication needs and appropriate assistance.
